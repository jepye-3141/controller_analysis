## Per-plan critique

### Plan A — BO/GP

- **Solves canonical problem?** Closely. Lines 12–15 reproduce the report's `J = E_ξ[Q] − λ E_ξ[U]` definition verbatim, with the right report cross-reference (lines 117–124). However, the plan implicitly identifies `Q(launch)` with the existing `landing_ratio` heatmap (line 17: "`R(x_t | x_d)` is implicitly built inside the function as `landing_ratio` (lines 254–256)"), without acknowledging that `landing_ratio` is the deterministic structural sweep, not `R(x_t | x_d)`. The pipeline section (line 45) eventually concedes that nuisance noise `(w_z0, w_y0)` must be sampled separately — which exposes the gap.
- **Method-citation alignment:** The KG citations (Frazier 2018; Pearce–Le 2024 *Engineering Optimization* 56(1) 36–53; Ungredda et al. arXiv 2209.15367) are correctly characterised as expectation-over-disturbances acquisitions. Pearce–Le robust-KG genuinely targets "objective is itself an expectation over uncertain ξ" — exactly this case (line 21). The MATLAB-`bayesopt` claim that custom acquisitions are forbidden (line 31) is correct.
- **Numerical claims:** Mostly grounded. "Tens to a few hundred" BO evaluations for d=4 (line 65) traces to Frazier and Bartoli et al. correctly. The `σ_Q ≈ √(p(1−p)/84) ≈ 0.05` figure (line 81) is right under a Bernoulli-per-cell model — but that is precisely the modelling step that conflates the 84 deterministic cells with i.i.d. samples (see "Pipeline understanding" below).
- **Pipeline understanding:** Partial. The plan reads `landing_ratio` as if each of the 84 cells were a Bernoulli sample drawn from `R(x_t | x_d)`. That is statistically wrong: each cell is a deterministic dSMC trial against a hand-picked (deploy, cross-track, neighbor) triple. The "MC noise on `Q`" the GP heteroscedasticity is supposed to model is therefore a structural-grid variance, not a sample variance. Line 81's `σ_Q` formula is suspect.
- **Notable mathematical concerns:** (i) The robust-KG Pearce–Le formulation assumes the disturbance `ξ` is sampled at evaluation time and the GP averages over it — not consistent with the deterministic 84-cell sweep. (ii) `p_target` is invented in the wrapper (line 27, "default = Gaussian centred on a user-provided point") with no attempt to surface where it comes from operationally — which is the same gap the report flags at line 119.

### Plan B — CEM

- **Solves canonical problem?** Identical formulation to Plan A (lines 12–15). Same canonical definition; same conflation of deterministic structural sweep with stochastic sampling. The plan's note that "each `J_i` already averages 84 inner trials" (line 88) is repeated in the risk discussion and in the FAQ — which means it is the load-bearing assumption that CEM noise modelling rests on, and it is wrong in the same way as Plan A.
- **Method-citation alignment:** Kobilarov RSS 2011, Kroese tutorial, iCEM (Pinneri et al. arXiv 2008.06389), Bregman-centroid CEM (Gu et al. arXiv 2506.02205) are all cited correctly as continuous CEM / multi-modal extensions. The "iCEM 2.7–22× sample efficiency for sequential control, not 4-D one-shot" caveat (lines 61, 87, 100) is responsible — it explicitly disclaims the speedup the report cites at line 78.
- **Numerical claims:** "`N_pop = 40, ρ = 0.2, generations ≤ 5`, total ≈ 6.7 h" (line 51) is internally consistent with the existing 2-min sweep wall.
- **Pipeline understanding:** Same deficiency as Plan A: 84-trial sweep treated as inner MC. The risk-#1 paragraph (line 76) claims vanilla CEM "does *not* model noise" — but the only noise is structural, so CEM never had to.
- **Notable mathematical concerns:** Less acute than Plan A because CEM does not impose Gaussian-noise assumptions on the objective the way KG does. CEM degrades gracefully under model misspecification; it just spends more budget.

### Plan C — PCE

- **Solves canonical problem?** Indirectly. Line 7 is candid: PCE is an *augmentation* of A or B, not a replacement, and only "becomes valuable if and when Plan A or Plan B introduces a true ξ-MC at the deployment-state level." Line 9 explicitly disclaims the wall-time benefit while the inner MC over ξ is absent. This is the most honest mathematical positioning in the six plans.
- **Method-citation alignment:** Xiu–Karniadakis 2002, Vittaldev–Russell–Linares JGCD 2016, Boutselis et al. arXiv 1705.05506, Witteveen–Iaccarino, Lüthen–Marelli–Sudret RSUQ-2020-002C are all cited correctly. The Wiener–Askey basis recommendation (Legendre for box-bounded, Hermite for Gaussian) is textbook. Compressed-sensing PCE (Adcock–Brugiapaglia–Webster, Peng–Hampton–Doostan) is correctly identified as the right tool for d=9.
- **Numerical claims:** "`d = 9, p = 3`: 220 terms; `p = 4`: 715" (line 26) is the correct combinatorial count `(d+p)!/(d!p!)`. The "10–100× speedup" claim from the report (line 84) is explicitly attributed to a *hypothetical* moments-only objective and disclaimed for the current pipeline. Excellent epistemic discipline.
- **Pipeline understanding:** Best of the six. Line 37: "`sweep_landing_centroid.m` is *deterministic in ξ*: it calls `eom2` exactly once at line 58 with the launch tuple, then sweeps the 8 deploy × 3 cross-track × 4 neighbor *structural* grid." Lines 41–42 articulate exactly the deterministic-vs-stochastic distinction the report's framing hides. The cost-accounting passage (lines 58–59) makes the dSMC-dominated bottleneck quantitative — `eom2` is 0.04% of wall — and is the only plan to do so.
- **Notable mathematical concerns:** None substantive. The non-smooth-output risk for Euler-angle channels (line 98) is correctly anticipated. Multi-element PCE is cited as a fix.

### Plan D — CVaR + cov-steering

- **Solves canonical problem?** Track 1 reformulates `E_ξ[Q]` as `CVaR_α(L)`, which is a principled risk-aware variant of the canonical objective (Rockafellar–Uryasev formula on line 17 is correct). Track 2 abandons the canonical objective in favour of certified-covariance bounds — but the plan correctly recognises that this is a different deliverable (line 9, "Track 2 is not justified by anything in the current research goal").
- **Method-citation alignment:** Rockafellar–Uryasev for sample-CVaR is correct. Deo–Murthy 2021 quantifying the O(1/√(αN)) tail-sample blow-up (line 27) is correctly used to bound the credibility of an `α=0.1, N=84` estimate. Tsiotras covariance-steering papers are cited but with the explicit disclaimer (line 58) that "there is no published precedent for covariance steering on a sliding-mode controller; the existing literature [...] linearizes around a nominal trajectory under affine state feedback, not a sliding-surface law." This is exactly the warning the judging prompt asks me to look for.
- **Numerical claims:** "9 samples is exactly the order at which sample-CVaR has an O(1/√(αN)) standard error of ~30–40%" (line 27) is correctly grounded in Glasserman et al. 2000. The Bentum–Asante BCa coverage claim (~95% at n<40, line 122) is appropriately hedged.
- **Pipeline understanding:** Best of the six (tied with Plan C). Lines 37–43 make the deterministic-vs-ξ point even more sharply than Plan C: "`landing_ratio` is *not* a Monte Carlo estimate of P[hit] in the statistical sense; it is the fraction of the 84-trial deterministic grid where dSMC stabilized." Line 41 correctly characterises a sort-and-mean over the cell-level miss distance as a "Conditional-Worst-Cell statistic, not a CVaR_α in the standard sense."
- **Notable mathematical concerns:** The Track-2 obstacle list (lines 56–60) correctly identifies that (i) dSMC saturation makes the closed loop non-smooth, (ii) ballistic dispersion enters as a 12-vector at apogee and propagates non-additively, and (iii) the SDP infrastructure is several months of work. Recommending `(i)` (empirical covariance bound) is the right pragmatic choice.

### Plan E — Multi-fidelity

- **Solves canonical problem?** Inherits Plan A or B's `J = E_ξ[Q] − λ E_ξ[U]` (line 10, "Let `J_hi(θ)` be the existing 84-trial dSMC sweep objective"). Does not address whether the 84-trial sweep is the right thing — accepts it from the upstream plan.
- **Method-citation alignment:** Le Gratiet & Garnier IJUQ 2014 / Kennedy–O'Hagan AR1 co-Kriging is the right model class; SMT 2.0 MFK is the correct implementation. MF-MES (Takeno et al. arXiv 1901.08275) and BOCA (Kandasamy et al. arXiv 1703.06240) are correctly characterised as `(θ, fidelity)`-joint acquisitions. The Spearman-`ρ_S ≥ 0.6` threshold (line 47) is correctly cited to *Nature Computational Science* 2025 best-practices paper, which the plan tracks down rather than fabricates.
- **Numerical claims:** The "200 mixed-fidelity in 40 min vs 20 high-fidelity in 40 min" sketch (line 70) is correctly debunked: at `ρ_S = 0.6` the AR1 posterior is dominated by the few HF samples, not the 200 LF. This is exactly the kind of numerical-claim grounding the judging prompt asks for. The 2-h saving over Plan A (line 64) is computed transparently from the budget.
- **Pipeline understanding:** Adequate. Recognises the dSMC sweep is the bottleneck (line 27, "the powered-phase reachability dominates the spread of `J(θ)`") and rejects 3-DoF point-mass as cheap fidelity for the right reason. But inherits the conflation of structural sweep with ξ-MC.
- **Notable mathematical concerns:** The `Q ∈ {0, hit}` cheap-fidelity output (line 59) is honest about its noise problem. Recommending `n_nb=4` for cheap-fidelity averaging (line 59) is reasonable. Local-correlation failure mode from Foumani et al. 2024 is correctly cited as a risk.

### Plan F — NN surrogate

- **Solves canonical problem?** Doesn't try to. Line 7 is unusually candid: "This plan is supervised regression on Monte-Carlo data, not certified Hamilton–Jacobi reachability." Line 7 also disqualifies DeepReach/CARe/NeuralPARC from the user's setting, which is the exact "DeepReach applied to MC reachability" failure mode the judging prompt flags. Plan F is best read as a caching-layer proposal, not an outer optimizer.
- **Method-citation alignment:** Bansal–Tomlin DeepReach, Chung et al. NeuralPARC arXiv 2409.13195, Solanki et al. CARe arXiv 2503.23912 are cited and explicitly marked "**Not applicable**" with the right reasoning (HJB residual or piecewise-affine dynamics required). The Devonport & Arcak attribution correction (line 28: "the paper is *Devonport & Arcak*, not Devolder/Hewing as in the lit-review draft") catches an actual literature-review error in the report. Author-correctness is excellent.
- **Numerical claims:** RMSE thresholds (line 59) are calibrated to the inherent inner-MC noise floor (`σ_reach ≈ 0.05`) rather than fabricated.
- **Pipeline understanding:** Recognises (line 33) that `Q` requires a `p_target` convolution downstream of the surrogate. Does not unpack where `p_target` comes from. Recognises the dSMC controller is under active development (line 71, "Plan A+ [...] is under active development; any change to the controller invalidates the cached") — a real reproducibility risk that other plans miss.
- **Notable mathematical concerns:** The cliff/saturation discussion (risk #2, line 68) correctly notes that ReLU MLPs interpolate across discontinuities and produce confidently-wrong predictions; the GP-vs-NN trade-off at small N is honest. The deep-kernel GP fallback (Profile BO arXiv 2512.23581) is appropriately positioned.

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **Deterministic-sweep vs random-noise.** Only Plans C and D explicitly identify that the existing 84-trial inner sweep is a deterministic structural grid (`8 deploy × 3 cross-track × 4 neighbor`), not an i.i.d. sample of `ξ`. Plan C: "`sweep_landing_centroid.m` is *deterministic in ξ*" (line 37). Plan D: "the current sweep has zero ξ-randomness inside it" (line 37). Plans A, B, E, F all silently assume the 84 cells are MC samples and use Bernoulli or similar variance models on top of them. This propagates into Plan A's heteroscedastic GP (line 20, "Bernoulli-style for `Q`") and into Plan B's noise-floor smoothing (line 76). The Plan A robust-KG acquisition assumes the noise is a sample average over a stochastic disturbance — at exactly the moment the plan's wrapper is averaging over a deterministic structural grid.

2. **Where does `p_target` enter?** The current pipeline has no `p_target` — it averages over the 84-cell heatmap as if each cell were equally important. The report (line 117) frames `Q` as a convolution `∫ R(x_t | x_d) p_target(x_t) dx_t`. Plan A invents `p_target` as a wrapper argument with a Gaussian default (line 27); Plans B and D accept this; Plan C does not address it; Plan E inherits Plan A; Plan F mentions a closed-form Gaussian convolution at inference (line 33). **No plan addresses how `p_target` is supplied operationally** — i.e., whether the user has a target distribution at all, or whether the existing power-weighted centroid (`p_centroid = 2`) is already the de facto risk-emphasised target. Plan D notices this in passing (line 131) and concludes Plan D's marginal value is therefore smaller than the report implies — a useful observation no other plan makes.

3. **Where does `U(x_d)` come from?** Plans A (line 18), B (line 49), and F (line 15) all correctly identify that `ctrl_trial` is the right source — currently consumed only for the TO_06 figure — and that `sweep_landing_centroid.m` needs a one-line patch to surface `U`. Plan D builds on the same patch (line 44 — note the patch is to line 233, surfacing miss-distance). Plan C does not address `U` at all (consistent with its scope being the ballistic kernel only). Plan E uses Plan A's `U`. The patch is small, but no plan has actually applied it.

## Recommendation

Plans **C** and **D** are the most mathematically aligned with the report's stated objective because they correctly diagnose that the existing 84-trial sweep is a deterministic structural grid rather than a Monte-Carlo sample of `ξ`, and they choose their scope accordingly (C: park unless ξ-MC is added; D: relabel the cell-level statistic as Conditional-Worst-Cell, not CVaR_α). Plans A, B, and E inherit the report's framing without challenging it and silently treat the 84 cells as i.i.d. samples — this subtly redefines the canonical objective from `E_ξ[Q] − λ E_ξ[U]` to a structural-grid average; Plan F sidesteps the question by repositioning itself as a cache. Plan A's robust-KG choice is the most acutely affected because its acquisition function explicitly assumes the noise it sees is a sample average over the disturbance.
