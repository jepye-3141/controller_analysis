# Monte Carlo Methods for Ballistic Targeting: A Literature Review for Inverse Stochastic Selection of Drone Deployment-Point Launch Parameters

## TL;DR
- The user's problem is best framed as a **two-stage stochastic optimization with recourse**: stage 1 (offline or precomputed) selects the four ballistic launch parameters (azimuth, elevation, velocity, spin), stage 2 (online) is the powered controller after deployment. The objective should be the **expectation of the convolution between the deployment-conditioned reachability heatmap and the target distribution**, penalized by expected control effort — this generalizes the precision-airdrop "Computed Air Release Point" (CARP) formulation of Rogers, Slegers, Leonard, Gerlach and Doman (AIAA JGCD 2017–2020) to a higher-dimensional ballistic decision space and a probability-of-hit objective.
- The recommended methodological stack pairs **GPU-accelerated 6-DoF Monte Carlo trajectory propagation** (Ilg–Rogers–Costello, AIAA AFM 2011; Trzun et al. 2020; Jacewicz–Głębocki 2023) with **Gaussian-process / Bayesian-optimization surrogates over the 4-D launch space** (Frazier 2018; Bartoli et al. 2024) and **active-learning refinement of the reachability surface** (Hewing et al.; Thorpe & Oishi 2019), optionally extended with **polynomial chaos expansion** (Vittaldev 2016; Boutselis et al. 2017) for analytical moment propagation. CEM/MPPI are *not* the right outer-loop tool here because they target online MPC, not offline launch-parameter selection — but they are good models for the *downstream* powered-flight controller whose performance is encoded in the reachability heatmap.
- Established methods exist for every component the user needs (CEP/dispersion ellipses, reachable-set footprints, hit-probability formulations, surrogate-based BO), but no single published pipeline solves the exact "ballistic-shell-deploys-then-powered-UAV" inverse problem with all four launch DoFs. The closest analog is **Leonard et al.'s Koopman/probabilistic CARP work for HALO airdrop with variable transition altitude** (JGCD 2020), which the user should treat as a primary template and extend to 4-D.

## Key Findings

1. **The user's problem is canonical two-stage stochastic optimization, but its specific 4-DoF ballistic-then-powered structure has no off-the-shelf solution.** The closest published analog is the Koopman-operator / probabilistic CARP work of Leonard, Rogers, Gerlach and Doman (AIAA JGCD 2017, 2019, 2020), which jointly optimizes release point, release heading, and parachute transition altitude under wind/dispersion uncertainty using Monte Carlo + score-function maximization. The user's problem replaces the parachute with a powered-controller reachability map, but the mathematical structure (maximize expected score = ∫ reachability(x; deployment) · target_pdf(x) dx − λ · effort) is identical.

2. **6-DoF Monte Carlo with drag, wind, and Magnus aerodynamics is the canonical fidelity tier for ballistic dispersion**; McCoy's *Modern Exterior Ballistics* (2nd ed.) remains the foundational reference, and modern engineering practice routinely uses 6-DoF MC for artillery, mortar, and reentry dispersion analysis (NASA Phoenix, MER, MSL EDL reports; Trzun et al. 2020 on manufacturing-error dispersion; Jacewicz & Głębocki 2023 on lateral-thruster guided projectiles). GPU-accelerated 6-DoF MC was demonstrated to be tractable for online use in airdrop guidance over a decade ago (Ilg, Rogers, Costello, AIAA AFM 2011; Rogers & Slegers, JGCD 2013, achieving real-time MC-ranked trajectory selection on a GPU). For the user's problem this provides an existence proof: even thousands of 6-DoF rollouts per candidate launch state are feasible in the offline pre-mission compute budget.

3. **Reachable-set footprints and probabilistic reachable sets are mature objects with multiple representations** (level sets, zonotopes, polynomial expansions, GP-classifier sub-level sets, neural-network surrogates). The Devolder–Singla / Hall–Singla "probabilistic reachability set" line (Penn State, 2019–2024) and Benito–Mease's reentry footprint work give the right vocabulary. The user's "reachability heatmap per deployment point" is a *probabilistic forward reachable set conditioned on a deployment state*, precisely the object Devolder & Singla call a probabilistic reach set or Thorpe & Oishi (CDC 2019) call a kernel-distribution embedding of the terminal-hitting probability.

4. **Probability-of-hit is the right objective, not miss distance.** The recent missile-guidance literature has been actively shifting from miss-distance minimization toward kill-probability/hit-probability maximization (e.g., the 2026 arXiv preprint "Kill-Probability-Maximization Guidance: Breaking from the Miss-Distance-Minimization Paradigm"). For the user's problem this is even more important because the deployment-conditioned reachability heatmap is *already* a probabilistic kill function, and the natural objective is its expectation under the target prior.

5. **For the outer optimizer over the 4-D launch space, Bayesian Optimization with a Gaussian-process surrogate is the dominant choice when each evaluation is an expensive Monte Carlo simulation.** Frazier's 2018 tutorial and the SEGOMOE / mixed-categorical BO frameworks of Bartoli et al. (ONERA, 2024) are directly applicable. For 4 dimensions, BO converges in tens to a few hundred MC-batch evaluations — well within budget. Knowledge-Gradient and robust-knowledge-gradient acquisition functions (Frazier 2018; Pearce, Branke, et al. 2022) are particularly suited because the user's objective is itself an expectation and exhibits stochastic noise.

6. **Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) and MPPI are widely misapplied to problems like this; they are the right tool for the *downstream* powered-flight controller, not for the launch-parameter selection.** CEM (Kobilarov 2011; Pinneri et al. iCEM 2020; CEM-GD 2021) and MPPI (Williams et al. 2017; Yin–Tsiotras CC-MPPI 2021; RA-MPPI 2022; C²U-MPPI 2025) are receding-horizon, sampling-based MPC methods that excel when many fast rollouts can be evaluated per control update. They could, however, be used to *generate* the reachability heatmap (i.e., they are the powered controller whose closed-loop hit rate is being summarized in the heatmap), and CEM specifically is a viable alternative to BO for the outer 4-D launch-parameter search if gradient-free, distribution-fitting search is preferred.

7. **Polynomial chaos expansion (PCE) and stochastic collocation provide an analytic alternative to brute-force MC** for propagating launch-parameter uncertainty through the ballistic phase. Vittaldev's *JGCD* 2016 work on Gaussian-mixture + PCE for spacecraft uncertainty propagation, the Boutselis–Pan–De La Torre–Theodorou 2017 gPC-DDP, and Nakka et al.'s gPC-SCP (2021) demonstrate that PCE can replace large MC ensembles when the input uncertainty is moderate-dimensional and the dynamics are smooth. For drag-affected ballistic flight with 4 launch DoFs plus wind and aero-coefficient uncertainty, sparse-grid stochastic collocation (Smolyak grids) typically beats MC for moments by 1–2 orders of magnitude in evaluations.

8. **Covariance steering (Tsiotras and collaborators) provides the rigorous link between launch-state distribution and terminal landing distribution under closed-loop control.** Ridderhof & Tsiotras's *JGCD* papers on Mars powered descent (2018, 2021, 2022) and chance-constrained covariance steering in random fields (JGCD 2022) show how to design a controller that *steers* the terminal covariance to a desired Gaussian — exactly the mathematical object that, when convolved with the target distribution, yields expected hit probability.

9. **Two-stage stochastic programming with recourse is the formal framework, but its structure should be exploited rather than solved directly.** Classical 2SP (Birge–Louveaux; recent surveys Frontiers Chem. Eng. 2021) is intractable for continuous control; instead, the user should *summarize* the recourse value (the expected hit probability achievable by the powered controller from a given deployment point) as a **value function over deployment state**, then optimize the launch parameters against the convolution of (a) the ballistic transition kernel from launch to deployment and (b) this value function. This is exactly the structure of Leonard et al.'s probabilistic CARP and of Gerlach–Doman's score-function airdrop optimization.

10. **Active learning / adaptive sampling is essential** because the 4-D launch space is too large to sample uniformly with expensive MC. Devolder, Hewing, Lewkowycz et al. (arXiv 1910.02500, "Data-Driven Reachable Set Computation using Adaptive Gaussian Process Classification and Monte Carlo") and Prajapat–Köhler–Lahrer 2025 (sampling-based GP-MPC with reachability) provide concrete adaptive algorithms that focus MC effort where the surrogate is most uncertain *and* where the objective is expected to be high — the latter is precisely the Expected-Improvement / Knowledge-Gradient acquisition principle.

## Details

### A. Monte Carlo Trajectory Propagation with Uncertainty for Ballistic and Projectile Flight

**Foundations.** Robert L. McCoy's *Modern Exterior Ballistics: The Launch and Flight Dynamics of Symmetric Projectiles* (2nd ed., Schiffer, 2012) remains the canonical reference for the 6-DoF dynamics, aerodynamic-coefficient databases, Magnus and pitch-damping moments, and aerodynamic jump that the user's ballistic-shell drone will experience. Carlucci & Jacobson's *Ballistics: Theory and Design of Guns and Ammunition* (CRC Press) is a complementary text. For circular error probable (CEP) and elliptical error probable (EEP), the foundational references are Grubbs (1964) and the U.S. Army Research Laboratory technical report ADA1043284 ("Circular Probable Error for Circular and Noncircular Gaussian Impacts"), which derives the Wilson–Hilferty approximation and quantifies its 0.47–2.17% error envelope.

**6-DoF Monte Carlo dispersion analysis** is industry-standard. NASA technical reports for Mars Pathfinder (Spencer & Braun, *Journal of Spacecraft and Rockets* 1996), MER (NASA NTRS 20080013365), Phoenix (NTRS 20080033126), and the X-43A hypersonic vehicle (NASA TM-2007-214630) establish the methodology: thousands of runs with dispersed initial state, atmospheric properties (Mars-GRAM, Earth-GRAM), winds, vehicle aerodynamic coefficients, and mass properties, post-processed for landing-ellipse statistics, peak-heating constraints, and parachute-deployment-condition compliance. For drag-affected projectiles specifically:
- **Ilg, Rogers, Costello (AIAA AFM 2011)**, "Projectile Monte-Carlo Trajectory Analysis Using a Graphics Processing Unit," is the seminal demonstration that GPU acceleration brings 6-DoF MC into the real-time regime for guidance.
- **Trzun, Vrdoljak, et al. (2020)**, "Monte Carlo Simulation of Missile Trajectories Dispersion due to Imperfectly Manufactured Warhead," combines a 3D CAD asymmetry model with 6-DoF MC.
- **Jacewicz & Głębocki (2023)**, "Analysis of Sounding Rocket Dispersion Using Monte-Carlo Simulation" and the parametric study of a 160 mm projectile with 34 lateral thrusters, are particularly relevant: they treat exactly the kind of pulsed-control, spin-stabilized, Magnus-affected projectile that resembles the user's ballistic shell.
- **Khalil, Rui et al. (2013)**, "Projectile Impact Point Prediction Based on Self-Propelled Artillery Dynamics and Doppler Radar Measurements," uses the Modified Point Mass model with explicit Magnus and spin-damping coefficients (Cnpα, Clp) — useful for the user's spin-rate launch variable.
- **Hainz & Costello (JGCD 2005)**, "Modified Projectile Linear Theory for Rapid Trajectory Prediction," is the canonical reference for *fast* (linear-theory-based) trajectory predictors that approximate 6-DoF MC for online guidance.
- **Eerland, Box, Sóbester (2017)**, "Cambridge Rocketry Simulator — A Stochastic Six-Degrees-of-Freedom Rocket Flight Simulator" (J. Open Research Software) is an open-source reference implementation.

**Reentry vehicle and hypersonic dispersion.** The X-43A MCAT framework (NASA TM-2007-214630), Mars Phoenix and MER reports cited above, and reentry footprint methods of Benito & Mease (Iowa State / UC Irvine, JGCD 2010 and follow-on) give the prototypical "footprint of reachable landing points" construction. The recent paper by Liu et al. on "Reentry Blackout Reachable Set Footprint Prediction Using Multi-Phase Trajectory Optimization" (*Acta Astronautica*, 2023) is an explicit modern example. For probabilistic propagation, Hall & Singla (Penn State, 2019) and Jain & Singla (AIAA SciTech 2023) develop sparse-collocation probabilistic reachable-set methods that are more efficient than vanilla MC.

**Spinning-projectile aerodynamics and Magnus-effect uncertainty.** The Magnus moment is small but, as McCoy emphasizes, decisive for stability and dispersion, and is one of the largest sources of uncertainty in ballistic MC. Recent CFD-based UQ work (Liu et al., Springer 2024, "Magnus Moment Calculation of M910 Spinning Projectiles"; *Int. J. Aero. Space Sci.* 2022 review on Magnus over body-fin configurations) uses DDES rather than RANS to capture unsteady separation. For the user's problem, the launch spin-rate variable directly modulates Magnus drift; treating the Magnus coefficient as an uncertain input in MC is standard practice.

**Reachable-set / footprint construction from MC.** The methods divide into:
1. **Convex hulls / α-shapes** of MC terminal points (simplest, used in Phoenix/MER analyses).
2. **Level-set methods** (Mitchell's HJB toolbox) — accurate but cursed in dimensionality.
3. **Zonotope and constrained-zonotope reachability** (Althoff and collaborators; Patel & Subbarao arXiv 2403.15294 for hypersonic reentry; arXiv 2601.17155 for low-thrust spacecraft).
4. **Polynomial / sensitivity-tensor expansions** (Singla and collaborators, Boone & McMahon JGCD 2024 "Directional State Transition Tensors").
5. **GP-classifier sub-level sets** (Devolder et al. arXiv 1910.02500, "Data-Driven Reachable Set Computation Using Adaptive Gaussian Process Classification and Monte Carlo").
6. **Christoffel-function support estimators** (arXiv 2112.09995).
7. **Neural-network surrogates** (DeepReach, NeuralPARC arXiv 2409.13195, CARe arXiv 2503.23912).

For the user's problem, the reachability heatmap is already in form (5)/(6) — a probabilistic occupancy function over terminal landing points conditioned on a deployment state — so the appropriate formal language is the *probabilistic forward reachable set* of Vinod & Oishi or the *terminal-hitting probability* of Thorpe & Oishi (arXiv 1908.00697, "Model-Free Stochastic Reachability Using Kernel Distribution Embeddings").

**Variance reduction.** Latin Hypercube Sampling (McKay 1979), antithetic variates, control variates, importance sampling, and Quasi-MC (Sobol, Halton sequences) are all standard. The Olsson–Sandberg ScienceDirect paper "On Latin Hypercube Sampling for Structural Reliability Analysis" reports >50% sample-reduction in importance-sampling settings. For *aerospace UQ specifically*, the Yao et al. *Progress in Aerospace Sciences* survey ("Review of improved Monte Carlo methods in uncertainty-based design optimization for aerospace vehicles," 2016) is the right starting point, and the Refined Stratified Sampling work of Shields et al. (arXiv 1505.02689) is state-of-the-art for adaptive UQ. For *rare-event* probabilities (e.g., probability-of-hit for narrow targets), Rao–Maulik–Constantinescu–Anitescu (arXiv 2006.03466) show that ML-based importance sampling biases the proposal distribution to the failure manifold.

**Applicability to user's problem.** Direct fit. The user's existing simulation already produces the reachability heatmap and control-effort surface — these are exactly the per-deployment-point statistics that 6-DoF MC dispersion analysis produces. The novelty for the user is *backward chaining*: rather than reporting CEP at the impact point given a launch state, one must propagate the *deployment-state distribution* through the ballistic phase and convolve with the reachability map. This is precisely the precision-airdrop release-point problem solved by Leonard–Rogers–Gerlach (see Section B).

### B. Stochastic Optimal Control and Belief-Space Planning for Terminal Targeting

**Chance-constrained MPC.** The CC-MPC literature (Schildbach et al. 2014; Margellos et al. 2014; Lew, Bonalli, Pavone — autonomous systems and aerospace) addresses constraint satisfaction with bounded violation probability. Recent work directly relevant: Ren–Ahn–Kamgarpour (arXiv 2401.03799, 2503.06779) on GMM-based CC-MPC; Chen et al. (arXiv 2106.02801) gPC-SCP for chance-constrained nonlinear stochastic systems; Zhang–Ma–Chen (arXiv 2504.04705) on chance-constrained trajectory optimization via set erosion; Pezzato et al. (CC-VPSTO, arXiv 2402.01370). For the user's problem, *control-effort budget as a chance constraint* (P[∫‖u‖²dt > U_max] ≤ ε) is a natural reformulation that converts the soft penalty on control effort into a hard probability constraint compatible with the rest of the chance-constrained formulation.

**POMDP / belief-space planning.** The state-of-the-art for target acquisition under target uncertainty includes:
- van den Berg et al., "Motion Planning Under Uncertainty for Medical Needle Steering Using Optimization in Belief Space" (IJRR / IROS), the canonical iLQG-in-belief-space paper that handles target uncertainty plus underactuated nonholonomic dynamics — directly analogous to the user's drone-to-target problem.
- Ahmadi et al. and Burks–Ahmed (arXiv 1807.08229) "Optimal Continuous-State POMDP Planning with Semantic Observations" for target search and interception with continuous-state, GM-belief representations.
- DESPOT, POMCPOW, and Rao-Blackwellized POMCPOW (arXiv 2409.16392) for online POMDP search.
- Reach-avoid POMDPs (arXiv 2603.10572) closing the loop between POMDP belief and reach-avoid certificates.

For the user's problem, treating the target distribution as the prior belief over a static (or slowly-moving) target is appropriate, and the powered controller's reachability map is exactly the belief-conditioned achievability function. If the user wants to handle target re-localization during flight, a POMDP formulation is warranted; if the target distribution is fixed at planning time, the simpler stochastic-program structure of Section A is sufficient and faster.

**SDDP / iLQG / Robust-iLQG.** Boutselis–Pan–De La Torre–Theodorou (arXiv 1705.05506), "Stochastic Trajectory Optimization for Mechanical Systems with Parametric Uncertainties," extends DDP via gPC and is well-suited to the moderate-dimensional uncertainty of the user's problem. Robust-iLQG (Sun, van den Berg, Alterovitz 2016; Lee–Lee CMU "GP-iLQG: Data-driven Robust Optimal Control"), Gramlich–Scherer–Ebenbauer's Robust DDP (arXiv 2205.12632), and Rahman–Waslander's Augmented-Lagrangian Belief-Space DDP (arXiv 2012.00218) are alternatives if a feedback policy in the powered phase is desired rather than open-loop control.

**Cross-Entropy Method.** Kobilarov, "Cross-Entropy Randomized Motion Planning" (RSS 2011, IEEE Xplore 6301069) is foundational. iCEM (Pinneri et al., arXiv 2008.06389) and CEM-GD (Huang et al., arXiv 2112.07746) are modern improvements with 2.7–22× sample efficiency gains. Bregman-Centroid Guided CEM (arXiv 2506.02205) addresses CEM's premature unimodal convergence. CEM is a very natural choice for the *outer* optimization in the user's problem: the launch-parameter space is 4-D, the objective (expected hit probability) is non-differentiable through the MC, and CEM's distribution-based sampling provides a natural way to amortize MC effort across a sequence of refining proposal distributions over (azimuth, elevation, velocity, spin).

**Probability-of-hit / kill-probability maximization.** Zarchan's *Tactical and Strategic Missile Guidance* (AIAA Progress in Astronautics & Aeronautics, 6th ed.) is the canonical reference for how miss-distance distributions translate to single-shot kill probability via lethality (cookie-cutter or smooth) damage functions. The 2026 arXiv preprint "Kill-Probability-Maximization Guidance" explicitly argues for using the smooth lethality function as the direct optimization objective rather than miss-distance proxies — this is precisely what the user's reachability heatmap is.

**Path-integral control / MPPI.** Williams, Aldrich, Theodorou (JGCD 2017) "Model Predictive Path Integral Control: From Theoretical Foundations to Real-Time Applications" is foundational. CC-MPPI (Yin, Zhang, Theodorou, Tsiotras, IEEE CDC 2021), RA-MPPI (arXiv 2209.12842), and U-MPPI / C²U-MPPI (arXiv 2501.08520) extend MPPI to chance constraints and CVaR. The MIT Sertac Karaman / Stanford Pavone groups have published Tube-MPPI variants. **For the user's problem, MPPI is the right architecture for the powered post-deployment controller** (it handles arbitrary cost functions and stochastic dynamics, and its rollouts naturally produce the reachability heatmap), but it is *not* appropriate as the outer launch-parameter optimizer because the launch decision is once-and-for-all rather than receding-horizon.

**Two-stage stochastic programming.** The textbook reference is Birge & Louveaux's *Introduction to Stochastic Programming* (Springer, 2011); for control-relevant connections, the Frontiers in Chemical Engineering 2021 review and Shapiro–Dentcheva–Ruszczyński's *Lectures on Stochastic Programming* are appropriate. The core insight for the user's problem is that the recourse value Q(x_deploy) — the expected hit probability achievable by the powered controller from deployment state x_deploy — should be precomputed as a function over deployment states (this is exactly the user's reachability heatmap, possibly weighted by the target distribution and the control-effort surface), and the first-stage problem becomes:

  max_{az, el, v, ω} E_{ξ}[ Q(F_ballistic(az, el, v, ω; ξ)) ] − λ · E_{ξ}[ effort(F_ballistic(az, el, v, ω; ξ)) ]

where F_ballistic is the stochastic ballistic transition map, ξ are the wind/aero/launch noise variates, and Q is the reachability-target convolution. This is the mathematical heart of the recommended approach.

**Guidance laws for ballistically deployed / air-dropped UAVs.** The most analogous prior work:
- **Leonard, Klein, Jumonville, Rogers, Gerlach, Doman, "A Probabilistic Algorithm for Ballistic Parachute Transition Altitude Optimization" (JGCD 2017)** and **"Koopman Operator Approach to Airdrop Mission Planning Under Uncertainty" (JGCD 2019)**, and **"Probabilistic Release Point Optimization for Airdrop with Variable Transition Altitude" (JGCD 2020)** — these jointly optimize *release point + heading + transition altitude* (3 DoFs) under wind and dispersion uncertainty using MC ensembles and a mission-specific score function. The Koopman variant pulls back the score function through a family of dynamics. **This is the paper family the user should clone-and-extend most directly.**
- **Rogers & Slegers, "Robust Parafoil Terminal Guidance Using Massively Parallel Processing" (JGCD 2013)** runs real-time GPU MC during terminal guidance for robustness ranking — directly transferable to the user's deployment-point selection.
- **Luders, Sugel, How, "Robust Trajectory Planning for Autonomous Parafoils Under Wind Uncertainty" (MIT, JGCD)** and Fowler & Rogers' Bézier path-planning JGCD paper give the planning-side counterpart.
- **GLMAV (Gun-Launched Micro Air Vehicle)** literature, particularly the Koehl–Boutayeb et al. ScienceDirect paper on hierarchical backstepping control of a GLMAV in crosswinds, describes a system that is functionally identical to the user's: shell-encased rotorcraft launched ballistically, deployed at apogee. The control hierarchy presented (ballistic → deployment → powered tracking) is exactly the user's architecture.
- **Tube-launched UAV literature** — Switchblade (AeroVironment), Coyote (Raytheon), ALTIUS-600/700 (Anduril/Area-I), Hero-series (UVision), and the MDPI 2024 paper "Investigation of a Tube-Launched UAV with a Variable-Sweep Wing" — describe the system class but are typically thin on the optimization formulation.
- **Sounding-rocket-deployed instruments and high-altitude sonobuoys** (Karatas et al. 2018 in *Naval Research Logistics*; sonobuoy patents) are weakly analogous: they treat the ballistic phase but typically without a powered terminal control phase.

**Applicability to user's problem.** Sections A and B together establish that the user's problem has a clear mathematical formulation (two-stage stochastic program with recourse value = expected hit probability) and a clear computational formulation (MC sampling of the ballistic transition × reachability heatmap evaluation × control-effort penalty), and that direct precedents exist in the precision-airdrop literature. The main gap is that no existing paper handles all four DoFs (azimuth, elevation, velocity, spin) simultaneously with a probability-of-hit objective and a control-effort secondary criterion.

### C. Surrogate-Model-Based Optimization Over Monte Carlo Data

**Gaussian process / Kriging surrogates.** The user's hit-probability and control-effort surfaces are smooth functions of (az, el, v, ω) once MC noise is averaged out, making them ideal Kriging targets. For ballistic-context UQ, key references:
- Vittaldev & Russell (JGCD 2016), "Spacecraft Uncertainty Propagation Using Gaussian Mixture Models and Polynomial Chaos Expansions."
- Boone & McMahon (JGCD 2024) on directional state-transition tensors.
- Hewing, Kabzan, Zeilinger, "Cautious Model Predictive Control Using Gaussian Process Regression" (IEEE TCST 2020) and Prajapat–Köhler–Lahrer (arXiv 2505.07594) on GP-based reachable-set MPC.

**Bayesian Optimization for expensive MC.** Frazier's 2018 tutorial (arXiv 1807.02811) is the standard reference; Shahriari et al. (Proc. IEEE 2016, "Taking the Human Out of the Loop") covers acquisition functions. Aerospace-specific BO:
- Bartoli, Lefebvre, Lafage, Saves et al., "Multi-objective Bayesian Optimization with Mixed-Categorical Design Variables for Expensive-to-Evaluate Aeronautical Applications" (arXiv 2504.09930) — SEGOMOE framework, ONERA, directly relevant.
- Yondo, Andrés, Valero, "A review on design of experiments and surrogate models in aircraft real-time and many-query aerodynamic analyses" (Progress in Aerospace Sciences 2018).
- Miller, Mak, Sun et al. (arXiv 2410.01196), "Expected Diverse Utility (EDU)" for control-strategy diversity in flight-propulsion engine BO.
- Tadeusz Pearce, Branke et al. (Tandfonline 2022), "Using the knowledge gradient acquisition function in Bayesian optimization when searching for robust solutions" — directly relevant because the user's objective involves expectation over uncertain ξ.
- Van der Herten et al. (arXiv 1608.04550), "Fast Calculation of the Knowledge Gradient for Optimization of Deterministic Engineering Simulations."

**Polynomial Chaos and stochastic collocation.** Xiu & Karniadakis (SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 2002, "Wiener–Askey Polynomial Chaos") is foundational. Aerospace UQ via gPC: Prabhakar et al. on hypersonic flight uncertainty; Kaltenbacher et al.; Witteveen–Iaccarino simplex stochastic collocation. Adaptive sparse-grid (Smolyak) collocation (arXiv 1509.01462, 1610.03863, 1811.00177) is appropriate for moderate-dimensional UQ such as the user's. Physics-constrained PCE (Sharma et al. arXiv 2402.15115) and the Robust MOEA-PCE work (arXiv 2205.11387) for trajectory optimization under wind uncertainty are recent advances. PCE-based DDP (Boutselis et al. arXiv 1705.05506) and gPC-SCP (Nakka et al. arXiv 2106.02801) directly couple PCE to trajectory optimization.

**Neural-network surrogates for reachability/capture sets.** DeepReach (Bansal & Tomlin), CARe (arXiv 2503.23912) for certified deep reachability, NeuralPARC (arXiv 2409.13195) for ReLU-based reach-avoid, and Xiang et al. (arXiv 2004.12273) for simulation-guided NN reachable-set estimation are state-of-the-art. For the user's 4-D launch-parameter problem, NN surrogates are likely *overkill* — a GP with ~200–500 MC-batch evaluations should suffice — but if the heatmap surface turns out to be highly non-stationary, a deep-kernel GP or DGP surrogate (arXiv 2512.23581, "Profile Bayesian Optimization for Expensive Computer Experiments") becomes attractive.

**Combining reachability heatmap + control effort + target distribution into a single objective.** The mathematically natural construction is:

  J(launch) = ∫_{x_d} p_deploy(x_d | launch) · [ ∫_{x_t} R(x_t | x_d) · p_target(x_t) dx_t ] dx_d − λ ∫_{x_d} p_deploy(x_d | launch) · E[u²|x_d] dx_d

Here p_deploy is the ballistic-phase forward density of the deployment state under MC (computable by KDE, GMM-fit, or PCE), R(x_t | x_d) is the user's reachability heatmap (probability-of-hit at landing point x_t from deployment state x_d), p_target is the target prior, and E[u²|x_d] is the control-effort surface. The inner bracket is exactly the **convolution of reachability with target distribution** that the user's problem statement asks about. This formulation appears in essentially this form in:
- Leonard et al. JGCD 2020 (probabilistic CARP score function).
- The kill-probability literature (Zarchan; arXiv 2604.17811).
- The information-theoretic search literature (Burks & Ahmed arXiv 1807.08229).
- POMDP terminal-cost formulations.

**Active learning and adaptive sampling.** The relevant techniques:
- **Adaptive GP classification for reachable sets** (Devolder et al. arXiv 1910.02500): refines the reach-set boundary where the GP is most uncertain.
- **Refined Stratified Sampling** (Shields et al. arXiv 1505.02689) and Hierarchical/Replicated LHS extensions: extend MC ensembles incrementally without re-starting.
- **Multi-fidelity BO** (Kandasamy et al.) when low-fidelity (3-DoF point-mass + drag) and high-fidelity (6-DoF + Magnus + wind) models are both available — directly applicable to the user's problem since cheap point-mass propagators can be used for screening.
- **Information-theoretic acquisition functions** (Hennig & Schuler "Entropy Search" JMLR 2012; Predictive Entropy Search Hernández-Lobato et al.; Knowledge Gradient Frazier 2018).
- **Trajectory-oriented BO for stochastic simulators** (arXiv 2510.18099) when the MC-noise structure should be explicitly modeled in the surrogate via random-seed-aware GPs.

**Computational tractability and amortization.** TOAST (Anderson et al., arXiv 2312.14336, JGCD 2025) and the Stanford "Deep Learning Warm Starts for Trajectory Optimization" work (arXiv 2505.05588) demonstrate that an offline-trained NN can map mission parameters to warm-start primal-dual solutions, accelerating online SCP/SQP solves by orders of magnitude. For the user's problem this enables: (a) precomputing the reachability heatmap and control-effort surface offline as high-resolution 4-D tensors or NN surrogates, then (b) doing the launch-parameter optimization online via fast surrogate evaluation rather than fresh MC.

**Applicability to user's problem.** Directly applicable. A GP/Kriging surrogate over the 4-D launch space, fitted to ~100–500 batch-MC evaluations of the expected-hit-probability objective, with EI or KG acquisition for the next batch, will likely converge to within fractions of a percent of optimum within a manageable compute budget. Active learning on the *reachability heatmap itself* (refining R(x_t | x_d) where the gradient of the objective with respect to x_d is largest) is a higher-leverage second-order improvement.

### Cross-cutting topics

- **Combining reachability + target distribution** is the convolution / inner-product construction shown above (Leonard et al. JGCD 2020; Zarchan; Burks–Ahmed). Implementation: pre-tabulate the convolution ∫R(x_t|x_d)p_target(x_t)dx_t as a scalar field over deployment-state space (the "target-weighted reachability"), then optimize launch parameters to maximize the *ballistic-stochastic expectation* of this field, minus λ × control-effort field.

- **Inverse problem (reachability-heatmap → launch state).** No closed-form inverse exists; the principled approaches are: (i) GP/BO over launch space with target-weighted reachability as objective; (ii) CEM over launch space; (iii) gradient-based optimization through a differentiable ballistic propagator (auto-diff) plus a smoothed reachability surrogate (NN or GP); (iv) Koopman-pullback à la Leonard et al. JGCD 2019.

- **Control effort as soft constraint / secondary objective.** Three principled formulations: (a) scalarized weighted-sum (λ as Pareto trade-off knob); (b) ε-constraint (max hit probability subject to E[effort] ≤ E_max); (c) chance-constraint (P[effort > U_max] ≤ ε). For decision-maker insight, multi-objective BO producing a Pareto front over (hit-probability, expected-effort) is highly recommended (Bartoli et al. 2024; Daulton et al. qNEHVI).

- **Online vs. offline.** Best practice: (i) offline, precompute the reachability-and-effort fields at high resolution and fit GP/NN surrogates; (ii) offline, build a 4-D launch-parameter surrogate and optimize via BO/CEM; (iii) online, given an updated target distribution, evaluate the precomputed surrogate at a few hundred samples plus a 1–10-step refinement — sub-second on commodity hardware. Warm-start methods (TOAST) further amortize.

- **Real-world analogs.** Switchblade 300/600 and ALTIUS-600/700 (tube-launched, but typically with rocket assist and not purely ballistic-then-powered), shell-deployed loitering munitions (USPTO 11,040,772; 11,753,160 — 40 mm grenade-launcher-deployed UAVs are a near-perfect match for the user's geometry), GLMAV (ISL Saint-Louis), JPADS / AGAS-2000 precision airdrop, Mars EDL parachute-deployment trigger optimization, and sonobuoy ballistic-then-parachute deployment (USPTO 3,978,790; Karatas 2018).

## Recommendations

The user should pursue the following methodological combinations, in priority order. Each combination is supported by published precedents and is feasible with the user's existing simulation infrastructure.

### Recommendation 1 (PRIMARY) — Probabilistic CARP-style optimization with GP/Bayesian-Optimization surrogate over the 4-D launch space

**Architecture:**
1. Use the existing simulation to pre-tabulate, on a dense (offline) grid in deployment-state space, the **target-weighted reachability** Q(x_d) = ∫R(x_t|x_d) p_target(x_t) dx_t and the **expected control effort** U(x_d).
2. Define the objective J(launch) = E_ξ[Q(x_d(launch, ξ))] − λ E_ξ[U(x_d(launch, ξ))], where ξ is the joint (wind, aero, launch-noise) uncertainty and x_d is computed by 6-DoF MC ballistic propagation.
3. Run Bayesian Optimization over (azimuth, elevation, velocity, spin) using a GP surrogate, with Knowledge-Gradient or robust-KG acquisition (Pearce & Branke 2022), and batch-MC evaluations (e.g., 256 trajectories per launch sample).
4. Either treat λ as a Pareto knob and produce a multi-objective Pareto front (Bartoli et al. SEGOMOE) or set λ via the ε-constraint method to a budget on expected effort.

**Supporting citations.** Leonard, Rogers, Gerlach, Doman JGCD 2017/2019/2020 (probabilistic CARP and Koopman release-point optimization); Frazier arXiv 1807.02811 (BO/KG); Bartoli et al. arXiv 2504.09930 (mixed-variable aero BO); Pearce & Branke (Tandfonline 2022, robust KG); Ilg–Rogers–Costello AIAA AFM 2011 (GPU 6-DoF MC); McCoy *Modern Exterior Ballistics* (ballistic dynamics).

**Decision criterion.** Choose this if total compute budget allows ~10²–10⁴ batch-MC evaluations of the objective and the launch-parameter space is moderate-dimensional (≤ 6).

### Recommendation 2 (ALTERNATE / COMPLEMENTARY) — Cross-Entropy Method over the launch space with a GPU-accelerated 6-DoF MC inner loop

**Architecture:**
1. Initialize a Gaussian distribution over (az, el, v, ω) covering the feasible launch envelope.
2. Each iteration, sample N_pop launches, run a moderate-size GPU 6-DoF MC ensemble per sample (e.g., 64–128 trajectories), score each launch by the same J(launch) above, retain the top elite fraction, refit the Gaussian (mean + diagonal/full covariance), and repeat.
3. Use iCEM-style temporally-correlated sampling and memory (Pinneri et al. arXiv 2008.06389) for sample efficiency, and Bregman-centroid guidance (arXiv 2506.02205) if multi-modal solutions are suspected.

**Supporting citations.** Kobilarov RSS 2011 (CE motion planning); Pinneri et al. iCEM (arXiv 2008.06389); Rogers & Slegers JGCD 2013 (GPU-MC for guidance); Williams et al. JGCD 2017 (information-theoretic MPPI/CEM connection).

**Decision criterion.** Choose this over BO if (a) gradient-free and easily parallelizable is preferred, (b) the objective surface may be multi-modal in the launch parameters (e.g., low-elevation/high-velocity vs. high-elevation/low-velocity solutions both viable), or (c) the simulator already has a GPU-batch interface.

### Recommendation 3 (HIGH-FIDELITY UQ AUGMENTATION) — Polynomial-chaos / stochastic-collocation surrogate for the ballistic transition kernel

**Architecture:**
1. Build a non-intrusive PCE expansion of the ballistic-phase deployment-state map x_d = F(launch, ξ) over the joint (launch, ξ) input space using Smolyak sparse-grid collocation or compressed-sensing PCE.
2. Use the PCE to compute moments and quantiles of x_d analytically, replacing the inner MC ensemble.
3. Plug the PCE-derived deployment density into the same outer optimization as Recommendation 1 or 2.

**Supporting citations.** Vittaldev (JGCD 2016, GMM+PCE); Boutselis–Pan–Theodorou arXiv 1705.05506 (gPC-DDP); Nakka et al. (arXiv 2106.02801, gPC-SCP); Sharma et al. (arXiv 2402.15115, physics-constrained PCE); Witteveen–Iaccarino simplex SC; Yao et al. *Progress in Aerospace Sciences* 2016 (improved-MC review).

**Decision criterion.** Adopt this *augmentation* once the launch-parameter space dimensionality is locked and the input uncertainty dimension is moderate (≤ 8–10); it can replace the per-launch MC inner ensemble with deterministic PCE evaluations and yield 10–100× speedup, freeing MC budget for the outer optimization.

### Recommendation 4 (RISK-AWARE / ROBUST EXTENSION) — CVaR/chance-constrained reformulation with covariance-steering analysis of the powered phase

**Architecture:**
1. Replace the expected hit probability with a CVaR_α-of-miss objective (or, equivalently, P[hit] ≥ 1 − ε) so the launch decision is robust to tail-event ballistic dispersions.
2. Independently, design the powered controller using Tsiotras-style covariance steering so that the *terminal* landing-state covariance is explicitly bounded; this gives a closed-form, certifiable upper bound on the reachability map's effective spread that the launch optimizer can rely on.

**Supporting citations.** Yin, Zhang, Tsiotras CDC 2021/2022 (RA-MPPI, CC-MPPI, covariance-steering MPPI); Ridderhof & Tsiotras JGCD 2018/2021/2022 (Mars covariance steering); Liu–Ma–Chen arXiv 2504.04705 (set-erosion chance constraints); Greco–Vasile (AIAA SciTech 2020) belief-stochastic optimal control for space trajectories.

**Decision criterion.** Adopt this if the application has *safety-critical* requirements where worst-case rather than average performance matters, or if the reachability heatmap from the user's controller exhibits long tails (rare large miss distances).

### Implementation roadmap

1. **Weeks 1–2.** Reformulate the problem mathematically as the two-stage stochastic program above, validate the convolution objective J(launch) on a few hand-picked launch states by reusing existing MC infrastructure, and verify that the existing reachability heatmap and effort surfaces are indexed by deployment state in a way that supports the convolution.
2. **Weeks 3–6.** Implement Recommendation 1 (BO over 4-D launch space with target-weighted-reachability objective). Use an off-the-shelf BO framework (BoTorch, GPyOpt, SMT for SEGOMOE). Validate on a synthetic test problem with a known optimum.
3. **Weeks 7–10.** In parallel, implement Recommendation 2 (CEM) as a verification baseline.
4. **Weeks 11–14.** Add Recommendation 3 (PCE) as inner-loop replacement for the ballistic MC if speedup is needed.
5. **Months 4–6.** Add Recommendation 4 if risk-awareness is a requirement.
6. **Throughout.** Maintain a small held-out set of "ground truth" launch states evaluated with very large MC ensembles (10⁴–10⁵ trajectories) to validate the surrogate-derived objective values.

## Caveats

- **The published precision-airdrop CARP work is the closest precedent but is in only 2–3 DoFs and uses parachute (not powered-controller) recourse.** The user's extension to 4 DoFs and a learned reachability heatmap is novel and may surface failure modes (e.g., highly non-stationary reachability surfaces near the boundary of the powered controller's authority) that are not addressed in the literature; plan validation experiments accordingly.
- **The reachability heatmap itself is presumably learned/simulated and therefore inherits noise and approximation error.** Active learning on the heatmap (Devolder et al. arXiv 1910.02500) is essential if the heatmap evaluation is itself expensive; the user should expose a "fidelity" knob in the heatmap evaluator and use multi-fidelity BO.
- **Magnus-effect modeling is the largest aerodynamic uncertainty for spinning ballistic shells** and CFD-based UQ literature (e.g., DDES vs. RANS results) shows that point estimates of Magnus coefficients can be wrong by 10–30%. Treat Magnus coefficients as uncertain inputs in MC, not as deterministic parameters.
- **POMDP-style closed-loop replanning during the powered phase** is not modeled in the simple two-stage formulation. If the powered controller can reactively re-localize the target (e.g., from an onboard seeker), the right framework is a partially-observable belief-space planner (van den Berg et al.; arXiv 1807.08229), and the recourse value Q(x_d) becomes the value function of the POMDP rather than a static reachability map — substantially more expensive but more accurate.
- **Some retrieved sources are speculative or non-peer-reviewed** (a few arXiv preprints from 2026 that have not completed peer review; vendor marketing pages for Switchblade and ALTIUS, which describe operational capabilities but not optimization formulations). The peer-reviewed JGCD, IEEE TAES, and *Automatica* citations and NASA/DTIC technical reports cited above are the load-bearing references.
- **Computational tractability claims (e.g., "BO converges in 10²–10³ evaluations") are typical for ≤ 6-D BO problems with smooth objectives** and stochastic noise on the order of a few percent; if the user's reachability surface is sharply ridged or near-discontinuous (e.g., catastrophic loss of authority at a deployment-state boundary), more sophisticated GP kernels (Matérn-1/2, deep kernels) or alternative surrogates (random forests, gradient-boosted trees) may be needed.
- **No single published reference covers the user's exact pipeline end-to-end.** This review's recommendations therefore necessarily synthesize results across the precision-airdrop, exterior-ballistics, stochastic-optimal-control, and surrogate-optimization literatures. Validation against the user's own simulation will be the ultimate arbiter.
